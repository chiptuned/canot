A departure rehearsal, in your accounts.

Your administrator sets up read-only access, then one command runs everything. You do not leave your provider, and you have nothing to request from them.

Everything happens in your accounts: your data platform, and the host you choose
  1. Set upA small open warehouse is created at the host you chose: one machine, one storage bucket, one catalog of Iceberg tables.
  2. CopyYour data is read with no ability to modify it, then written in Iceberg format to another of your accounts.
  3. ReplayThe 20 most important queries, chosen with you, run on both sides.
  4. CompareTables and results are compared with tolerances written in advance: row counts and per-column statistics are recomputed in the source engine, and a content fingerprint compares the rows as Canot read them with the copy.
  5. SignAn evidence file is produced: parity, duration, cost, and what does not move as is.

Canot sends nothing to Q4O: no telemetry, no server.

  • canot scanFree read-only X-ray: volumes, objects that do not move on their own, estimated exit duration and cost.
  • canot drillThe full drill and the evidence file.
  • canot teardownDeletes what was copied.

Platforms.

The scan and the exit drill work today on Snowflake and BigQuery. Others are added with a design partner: the connector is written and validated with you, on your account.

Exit locally, to your own infrastructure.

No cloud needed at the destination: a folder is enough. Canot writes the copy in Apache Iceberg format to your disk, your NAS or an S3 storage you host, with the evidence file next to it.

uvx canot drill "snowflake://MY_ACCOUNT/MY_DATABASE?user=MY_USER" /data/iceberg
  • /path/to/folderLocal disk, network share or mounted NAS.
  • s3://bucket/canot?endpoint=https://s3.internal:9000Your S3-compatible storage (MinIO, Ceph, NetApp…), with an internal proxy and certificate authority if needed.
  • --catalog env:CANOT_CATALOG_URIA PostgreSQL catalog of your own; the password stays in an environment variable.

The copy does not depend on Canot: DuckDB, Spark and Trino read it, and the reread.sql file recounts every table without the tool. Details are in the documentation.
