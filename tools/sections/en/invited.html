The problem

Moving onto a data platform is easy: vendors give you the tools. Checking that you can move off it is another matter. Regulators now ask for it (DORA requires a tested exit plan for critical ICT services, the Data Act requires exit clauses in cloud contracts), yet an exit plan on paper has never been executed: its duration, its cost and its gaps stay unknown until the day you need it.

What Canot does

1. Scan

Read-only inventory of what you run: tables, volumes, queries, costs, and what will not move as is. Nothing is changed on your side.

2. Copy

Your data is copied to the destination you choose (a European host, or your own premises) in the open Apache Iceberg format. DuckDB, Spark or Trino read it: no dependency on Canot.

3. Compare and replay

Row counts and per-column statistics are recomputed in the source engine and compared with the copy. Your most expensive queries, and your dbt models, are replayed on the copy.

4. Evidence

A one-page report (verdict, what moves as is, what to fix) backed by a signed, hash-chained log that makes any change visible. Each table states its proof level.

What Canot is not

It is not a certification and not legal advice: it produces a dated record of measurements on the scope you ran. It does not compare every value one by one (the report says exactly what it proves and what it does not). It never needs write access to your platform, and the free tool sends nothing to us.
